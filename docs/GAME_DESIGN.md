# Game design

## Identity

An embodied historical biography, not a general empire simulator with Garibaldi substituted into the portrait slot. The proposed progression changes responsibilities rather than replacing the person with a map cursor.

The sister-project relationship to 1792 is about persistent consequences, situated knowledge and gradual command. This game needs a maritime and volunteer-centred identity of its own. No Punjab-specific dynasty system, protagonist-specific eyesight condition or historical faction is imported automatically.

## Immediate player loop

Leave the starting point → find the dock contact → accept work → cross the harbour → obtain papers → deliver them → return → see persistent recognition.

The loop tests spatial readability, interaction feedback, a small amount of consequence and save/load. All NPCs are static placeholders. There is no simulated occupation, historical voyage or autonomous shipping economy behind the boxes.

## Later design pillars

**An embodied life.** Walking, riding, sailing, shelter, fatigue and relationships should anchor later military responsibilities. Proposed ageing and injury features must follow sourced chronology where presented as biography. Accessibility controls must not be disguised as a historical claim.

**A mobile base rather than a permanent capital.** A vessel, camp, safe house or temporary headquarters can anchor a region. Losing one should alter access and supply without requiring a second disconnected world simulation.

**People join for reasons.** Volunteers and companions need motives, commitments, local ties and limits. A person can support an operation without endorsing every political aim. Anita's eventual role needs her own research and agency; she is not just a romance reward.

**Information has a journey.** Intelligence quality, visibility, delay and custody matter. The player should not receive global knowledge merely because a faction exists in the simulation. Conversely, a rumour should not overwrite the actual state.

**Success generates pressure.** Raids and victories can improve recruitment while producing scrutiny, reprisal, supply strain or counter-coalitions. Define these as competing systems, not a single universal popularity or aggression bar. They are future mechanics, not equations proven by the scaffold.

**Command remains situated.** A selected subordinate operation may become a smaller playable story. It must return effects to the same persistent world, under an explicit command relationship, with no duplicate inventory or timeline. This is planned, not implemented.

## Biographical boundaries

The proposed default is a biographical campaign: document major historical outcomes, and place agency in routes, preparation, conduct, local relationships and consequences within those boundaries. There is no implemented historical-event scheduler yet. A counterfactual mode would need separate rules and visible labelling; it must not quietly rewrite the biography.

## Out of scope for the foundation

Multiplayer, a seamless transatlantic world, procedural full-life narrative generation, production naval combat, AAA traversal, automated historical truth scoring, a new shared engine and an army simulation are not foundation deliverables. Build one small playable behaviour at a time.
