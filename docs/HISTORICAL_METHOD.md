# Historical method

## Four distinct labels

| Label | Meaning | Prototype use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `documented_history` | A specific claim supported by identifiable evidence | Not assigned to the fictional errand |
| `reasonable_reconstruction` | An explicit inference consistent with evidence | Requires stated assumptions before use |
| `gameplay_abstraction` | A deliberate simplification or authored organization | Campaign chapter boundaries and themes |
| `fictional_connective` | Invented material joining the experience | Harbour layout, errand, contacts and dialogue |

The prototype's 1824 period anchor is supported by Garibaldi's early maritime biography. The work errand is not. A source citation beside a mixed record does not make every detail in that record factual.

## Initial research sources

1. Giuseppe Monsagrati, **GARIBALDI, Giuseppe**, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*, volume 52 (1999), Treccani. Use for broad chronological orientation and references to further scholarship. The opening paragraphs discuss the maritime beginnings. The prototype does not reproduce the biography's prose.
2. Brown University Library, **The Garibaldi & the Risorgimento Archive**. Use for research discovery and analysis of historical representation. Contemporary imagery is evidence about its creation and reception as well as its subject; it is not an unmediated photograph of all depicted events.

Full source URLs and limitations are in `data/history/sources.json`. No portraits, panorama images, archive scans or extracts are bundled. Source access does not automatically establish asset redistribution rights.

## Record discipline for later work

A historical claim should have a stable ID, precise text, applicable date or interval, region, source ID, locator (page, section or catalogue entry), classification, uncertainty note and competing-source notes. Confidence belongs to the claim, not to the prestige of a character or publisher. An author's account of their own actions is still an account with a viewpoint.

Do not assert a historically settled detail merely because it appears repeatedly in unsourced summaries. Do not project present-day borders, flags, uniforms, language or political institutions backward without checking the scene date. Do not make all people in one region share a single political view.

## Biography and agency

Major outcomes proposed as fixed campaign anchors must be reviewed and sourced before code enforces them. Moment-to-moment agency can concern preparation, routes, negotiation, care for companions, extraction and the survival of fictional participants. Distinguish a changed local consequence from a changed historical outcome. The current errand has no such fixed-event machinery.
