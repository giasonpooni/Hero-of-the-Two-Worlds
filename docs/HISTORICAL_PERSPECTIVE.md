# Connected lives and situated historical perspective

Decision: September 29, 2026. This is a narrative/production contract, not a new
playable feature. The existing harbour scaffold and fictional errand remain.

## Garibaldi as an entry point

The design uses Garibaldi's trajectory as an anchor for discovering connected
people, places, events, correspondence and competing accounts. Another person
should first be encountered through a situation, relationship or consequence,
not reduced to a biography card. A lesser-known figure may support an encounter,
a chapter or eventually a separate campaign; discovery is not automatic approval
for full production. Actual historical connections require evidence. Sharing a
time period or movement is not evidence that two people met.

Represent research as evidence-linked claims and relationships; author playable
trajectories through that material. Keep the modeled world, what each actor can
observe, what they believe, what they later remember, how an account is retold
and what a retrospective researcher knows distinguishable. The protagonist must
choose before receiving the outcome, not speak with the textbook's hindsight.

This is constrained historical theory-of-mind, not a claim to know anyone's true
private thoughts. Goals, motives and private conversations retain their own
attested/inferred/reconstructed/fiction status. Documented words do not prove
sincerity; a plausible simulated decision does not authenticate a historical
motive. Conflicting accounts need not receive equal evidentiary weight.

## Shared authoring tool, not a new engine

[Terminal PR #70](https://github.com/giasonpooni/Notations-Systems-Terminal/pull/70)
publishes the first Python tool at `e1fa85f89ff3f39db84ee9b157929133de49b72c`.
[Installation and full limits](https://github.com/giasonpooni/Notations-Systems-Terminal/blob/e1fa85f89ff3f39db84ee9b157929133de49b72c/docs/HISTORICAL_PERSPECTIVE.md).

`net history` projects actor-specific received information and audits annotated
statements through the original NET/CIW Session and registries. It supports
logical delivery times, conflicts, source-class checks, forecast/report
separation and one explicit belief-about-another-actor attribution.

It does not parse free-form prose, authenticate historical evidence, bind dates,
simulate communication geography or implement a live Godot integration. It is
not currently attached to this game's saves, journal or mission state. The
filtered actor export is distinct from the full operator research/workspace;
restrict worker access to that export rather than assuming JSON filtering is
an operating-system sandbox.

## First transfer test: still to implement

After the 1792 adapter is qualified, attach one bounded harbour message scenario
to this game's existing state transitions and clock. Use original fictional
contacts rather than inventing an attested historical meeting. A dock contact,
carrier and captain receive different evidence about the same event.

Require actual delivery before recipient knowledge, a missing/interrupted
message that grants nothing, contradictory accounts preserved, no repeat reward,
and save/load of pending delivery and received accounts. Switching viewpoint
must not transfer another person's private journal or create a second clock.
A forecast may be spoken as a forecast but not laundered into an observed fact.
Repeated relays of one source are not independent corroboration.

Run the same shared authoring contract, not a renamed copy of the runtime.
Game-owned adapters must declare source identity and clock mapping. Independent
narrative review still checks whether text matches its annotations and whether
the result is engaging. A passing data check is neither historical proof nor
human playtesting. No native or cross-title execution is claimed by this document.

## Portfolio and ownership

1792 remains the primary production workload. Hero of the Two Worlds develops
slowly as the secondary title and shared-workflow transfer test. Geronimo is on
hold. A growing historical graph is a research/design resource, not a commitment
to build every discovered character's campaign immediately.

Notations Game Foundry is a workload on the existing terminal, not a new engine
or replacement workbench. Godot retains this game's state, clock and saves;
Blender, optional Bevy and the intended C++/Rust/Python/Julia responsibilities
remain separately scoped. Existing native/provider integration requirements are
not promoted into implemented dependencies. No game code is copied into NET,
no licence is changed and no main or parallel development branch is merged.

Judge reuse by accepted integrated playable output per human hour, including
research, setup, supervision, cost, repair and visual/narrative review. Shared
scripts alone do not demonstrate that a second game becomes cheap to produce.
